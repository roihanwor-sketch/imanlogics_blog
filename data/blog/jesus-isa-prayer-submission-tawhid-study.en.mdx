## Unsealing Historical Witnesses: Faith, Logic, and Textual Transmission

In the silence of the Garden of Gethsemane on a fateful night, canonical Gospel records describe Jesus falling with his face to the ground in intense prayer...

Examining historical records of how prophets prayed—from Abraham and Moses to Jesus—reveals an unbroken continuum of radical submission (Islam).

Is the term "Muslim" merely a modern sectarian identity, or the core existential attitude of all prophetic surrender to God?

Beneath the sands of historical inquiry and interfaith dialogue, rigorous investigation into ancient manuscripts and theological logic provides invaluable insight into how humanity conceptualizes total surrender to the Creator.

### I. Historical Reconstruction & Primary Documentary Evidence

Textual analysis of ancient Greek manuscripts (Codex Sinaiticus, Vaticanus) on Matthew 26:39 establishes the phrase "epesen epi prosōpon autou" (fell upon his face).

Prostration is consistently documented in the Pentateuch (Genesis 17:3, Numbers 20:6) and Gospels (Luke 6:12, Matthew 26:39).

3rd-century papyri (P45, P75) and 4th-century uncial codices confirm the antiquity of these prayer narratives.

### II. Core Manuscript Witnesses & The Scribal Landscape

#### 1. Matthew 26:39 (Gethsemane) (Matt-26-39)

*Estimated Date:* 1st Century CE

Records Jesus falling prostrate, praying that the Father’s will be done rather than his own.

Semitic linguistic heritage (Hebrew Shalah, Aramaic Tslotha, Arabic Salah) inextricably links inner devotion with physical prostration.

---

### III. Scholarly Debates, Counterarguments & Opposing Views

Christian theology interprets Gethsemane as the submission of Jesus’ human will to the divine will within the Trinitarian framework.

Historical-Critical scholarship (Geza Vermes, Bart Ehrman) situates Jesus as a Jewish prophet preaching uncompromising submission to God alone.

Scholarly consensus affirms that the linguistic concept of "submission to God" represents the universal core of Semitic monotheism.

### IV. Conceptual Precision: Monotheism, Prophecy, and Worship

Islam defines "Muslim" ontologically: anyone who submits their will completely to Allah, as articulated by Jesus’ disciples in Surah Ali ‘Imran [3]: 52.

Jesus in Islam is the Messiah and noble Messenger sent to the Children of Israel to revive pure monotheism and elucidate the Law.

Prophetic worship is never mere legal formalism, but the harmonious fusion of transcendent monotheism and universal compassion.

---

### V. Evidence Matrix & Epistemological Demarcation

- **[FACT]** Canonical New Testament manuscripts (Matthew 26:39) record Jesus falling prostrate in prayer at Gethsemane. *(Source: [Codex Sinaiticus Project](https://www.codexsinaiticus.org))*

- **[ISLAMIC_INTERPRETATION]** Islam interprets Jesus’ prostration as an authentic act of prophetic worship rendered exclusively to Allah. *(Source: [Tafsir Ibn Kathir (Surah Ali Imran 3:52-84)](https://quran.ksu.edu.sa))*

- **[COUNTERARGUMENT]** Mainstream Trinitarian theology interprets Jesus’ prayer not as creaturely subordination, but as the hypostatic obedience of the Son to the Father. *(Source: [Oxford Dictionary of the Christian Church](https://academic.oup.com))*

- **[UNCERTAINTY]** The precise liturgical steps of prayer practiced by pre-Islamic prophets are not exhaustively preserved in extant manuscripts beyond general postures. *(Source: [Encyclopaedia of the Qur'an (Brill)](https://referenceworks.brillonline.com))*

### VI. The Islamic Epistemological Framework: Revelation Continuity & Transmission

The Qur'an unequivocally establishes that the foundational religion of all prophets is one: Al-Islam (absolute surrender to God).

Investigating biblical records reveals enduring traces of ancient monotheistic devotion and physical prostration practiced by prior prophets.

In **Surah Ali ‘Imran [3]: 84**, sacred scripture articulates this foundational principle:

> **"قُلْ آمَنَّا بِاللَّهِ وَمَا أُنزِلَ عَلَيْنَا وَمَا أُنزِلَ عَلَىٰ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَإِسْمَاعِيلَ وَإِسْحَاقَ وَيَعْقُوبَ وَالْأَسْبَاطِ وَمَا أُوتِيَ مُوسَىٰ وَعِيسَىٰ وَالنَّبِيُّونَ مِن رَّبِّهِمْ لَا نُفَرِّقُ بَيْنَ أَحَدٍ مِّنْهُمْ وَنَحْنُ لَهُ مُسْلِمُونَ"**
> 
> *Translation: "Say, "We have believed in Allah and in what was revealed to us and what was revealed to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and the Descendants, and in what was given to Moses and Jesus and to the prophets from their Lord. We make no distinction between any of them, and we are Muslims [submitting] to Him.""*

Ibn Kathir expounds that this verse establishes the universal brotherhood of all prophets; though secondary laws varied by era, their foundational creed was invariably Tawhid.

Reflecting on Jesus in prostration invites every earnest seeker to the core of worship: humble surrender before the Creator.

### VII. Intellectual Boundaries: What This Does—and Doesn't—Prove

A rigorous inquiry must maintain strict demarcation between empirical data and theological interpretation:

WHAT IT PROVES: Physical prostration and total submission are historical hallmarks of prophetic prayer. WHAT MUST NOT BE CLAIMED: One must not make crude anachronistic claims without rigorous semantic and contextual framing.
